## Verdict after three rounds

Scores went from 3 to 5 in round 1 to 4 to 5 in rounds 2 and 3. The round 1 fixes (grounding, driver hardware, framing) moved the needle; rounds 2 and 3 asked for the same things on every shot: penumbra that widens with distance, paper fibre and ink grain, floor reflections, bounce light, and one light the whole frame obeys. Those are properties of a path-traced render or a photoreal pass, not of the real-time renderer in `render/`, which is the limit `CLAUDE.md` names. The geometry never came up: proportions, bowl, drivers and label were accepted as drawn in every round.

What the critic asked for that is still cheap and render-side, for the next camera pass: move the hero's left speaker off the room's corner line, add skirting and a larger floor to the desk set, keep the lifted lid off the horizon line in the swap, and offset the close-up camera a few degrees so the faceplate shows its edge.

What needs the photoreal pass: everything about surface and light. The frames in `renders/2026-10-01` are proportion-exact control images for it.

## Round 4, on the locked look (v4)

A fourth round because the look changed (printed lids on the white flavours, the band with the wordmark, nothing under the gable); the three-round cap counts per look. Scores held at 3 to 5. The new print was accepted without comment in every shot: no critic flagged the band, the lid colour or the wordmark position, which is the result that mattered for this round. The asks are the ones from rounds 2 and 3 (one committed light, paperboard, bounce, penumbra), which the real-time renderer does not do.

Cheap carries for v5, all camera or placement: hero fill down so the sun's shadows read; night key rotated off the hero face; close-up bowl with a soft terminator and the top lip out of frame; lineup from further back on a longer lens; crate camera higher and closer so the woofers stop reading as wheels, planks with the wall; the Whole lid moved clear of the standing tube in the swap's third frame.
