## Verdict after three rounds

Scores went from 3 to 5 in round 1 to 4 to 5 in rounds 2 and 3. The round 1 fixes (grounding, driver hardware, framing) moved the needle; rounds 2 and 3 asked for the same things on every shot: penumbra that widens with distance, paper fibre and ink grain, floor reflections, bounce light, and one light the whole frame obeys. Those are properties of a path-traced render or a photoreal pass, not of the real-time renderer in `render/`, which is the limit `CLAUDE.md` names. The geometry never came up: proportions, bowl, drivers and label were accepted as drawn in every round.

What the critic asked for that is still cheap and render-side, for the next camera pass: move the hero's left speaker off the room's corner line, add skirting and a larger floor to the desk set, keep the lifted lid off the horizon line in the swap, and offset the close-up camera a few degrees so the faceplate shows its edge.

What needs the photoreal pass: everything about surface and light. The frames in `renders/2026-10-01` are proportion-exact control images for it.

## Path-traced pass, round 1 (v11-pt)

The first round on frames from Blender's Cycles (`render/pathtrace.py`), the same model and rig exported from the real-time scene. A new render path, so the count restarts. Scores 6, 5, 5, 4, 5, 5, 4: the hero's 6 is the highest any frame has scored, and for the first time no critic asked for penumbra, bounce or contact occlusion as such. What every report said instead was the same two things. The fill swamped the key: the dome I had given Cycles was about five times the real-time hemisphere once units are counted properly (an irradiance is π times a dome radiance), so every face sat at one value and nothing sat on the floor. And the lacquer had nothing to reflect: a uniform grey dome, razor arrises, a studio with a hard horizon seam. Two things were plainly bugs: the sun wash high on the hero's wall was the sun spilling over a window wall with no ceiling, and the black band under the crate was its base board sharing the walls' outer planes, which a path tracer renders black.

v12 answers all of that without touching the product: the three rooms closed with a ceiling and two more walls, so daylight is only what the window admits; light units converted (sun as irradiance, area lamps as radiance times area times π, points times 4π, the dome divided by π); the studio floor sweeping up into a backdrop, its key a softbox and its dome a gradient; the bronze rendered as a metal; the cabinet's arrises eased by about a millimetre in the shader, silhouette unchanged; the crate base inset. Three critic notes are not critic fixes and go to the owner: the spout reading as a lens barrel (02b), the crate wall hiding the drivers (06), and the chair as a massing model (02a) if the room is to be dressed.

## Path-traced pass, round 3 (v13-pt): the cap

Scores 5, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 5. Three rounds on the path-traced look have now been run on every shot, so this is the last word on these frames in this batch. The arc: round 1 (6, 5, 5, 4, 5, 5, 4) found a dome that swamped the key and nothing for the lacquer to reflect; round 2 (5, 6, 5, 5, 5, 6, 5) found the sun on the faces the camera could not see and the studio still dome-lit; round 3 found, with the windows moved to the camera's side and the studio keys raked, the next layer down, and it is a plateau rather than a slope. What round 3 names, and what it would take:

- **Exposure, both ways.** In the rooms the white body sits on the shoulder (231 on both faces of the apartment's cabinet, so the faces do not separate); in the studio it sits at 189 with nothing above 204. Raise the sun, whiten it so the floor stops going orange, push the band a metre back.
- **Contact and weight.** Big soft fills (the sky through a 2.6 m window, the studio dome even at fill strength) lift the contact line under every base. Halve the studio dome again and darken its backdrop a stop; in the rooms the fix is the exposure above.
- **The set.** The chair is a massing model, the floors are flat fills with hairline seams, the desk is the crate's own colour. A chair that is furniture, a darker desk top, grain that reads at room distance.
- **Beyond render, for the owner.** The spout reads as a lens barrel from across a room (every round, both rooms). The crate wall hides the pints' drivers. The Chocolate wordmark is polished metal on a cream plinth. The tweeter sits in the bowl with no visible flange. The close-up's camera shows only the gable's lip. The old room's lamp lights the product from out of frame. None of these is a critic fix.

Where it stands: the path-traced frames are the best this project has made, and no critic in three rounds asked for penumbra, bounce or a horizon again; what they ask for now is a photographer's second day on set. The real-time frames remain the layout check and the canvas the source of truth for layout, copy and proportions.
