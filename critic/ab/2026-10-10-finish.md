# A/B, 2026-10-10: the finish (a white point, contrast and sharpening in post)

## Why

Blind scores had sat at 5 to 6 for days. Each critic round's prescriptions were carried out and their tests passed,
yet the next critic's blind score did not move. The blind impressions had one complaint in common on almost every
shot: the white lacquer reads as flat, light grey. The frames bear it out. The white paint's lit face sat at 205 to
218 of 255 on every shot, and under 0.4 % of any frame was above 235. The critics' own tests had held the faces
under about 216 to keep their gradients out of the view transform's shoulder.

A photographer sets the white point in post. This A/B asks whether doing the same lifts a blind score.

## How

The judge sees two versions of one frame: the frame as rendered (retouched) and the same raw frame with a finish.
It is the creative-director prompt in `PROMPT-AB.txt`. The two are one render graded two ways. The judge does not
know which is which: the order was drawn at random, then balanced across judges (`finish-*.order.json`). Each judge
is a fresh agent that has seen nothing else.

- **finish 1** (`finish1.py`): the white paint's 97th, then 75th, percentile of luminance taken to 244, then 238;
  an exponential shoulder; S 0.15; clarity 0.08; sharpening 0.5 at 0.8 px. It is then re-retouched so the paints
  stay on their swatches.
- **finish 2** (`studio/engine/finish.py`, the engine's version): the white paint's 75th percentile to 236, with the
  gain capped at 1.12. A rational shoulder to a peak of 251, so nothing clips. The gain is lowered where it would
  take a channel past the peak, which keeps saturated colours. Contrast 0.12 and clarity 0.08. Sharpening 0.35 at
  0.7 px, cored at 2 levels and clamped to each pixel's 3 x 3 range, so there are no halos.

## Results

| frame | finish | finished | as rendered | prefers | what the judge named |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04b-e18 | 1 | **7.0** | 6.0 | finished | the carton reads white (232-238 against 204-210), the type crisper, the set airier; against it: the top of the face clipped (5,267 px at 253+), 1 px halos, the LED bleached, grain up 40 % |
| 09-e13 | 1 | **6.2** | 5.3 | finished | "B's tones never reach white ... the whole frame looks veiled"; the wordmarks crisp, the chrome lively; against it: halos round the surrounds |
| 04b-e18 | 2 | **6.5** | 5.5 | finished | white body, a stronger step against the backdrop (118 against 99), the type whiter, nothing clipped; against it: the top of the face a near-white plateau (249-250) |
| 02a-e10 | 2 | **6.5** | 5.5 | finished | "a real white point", the floor golden, the vase white ceramic; against it: the sun patch on the wall squashed into 239-241, the skirting merging with the wall |
| 10-e10 | 2 | **6.4** | 5.0 | finished | the faces white without clipping (0.05 % at 248+), the woofer's highlight a crisp metallic sweep; against it: the top of the red tower's face flatter (17 levels against 26) |

All five judges preferred the finished frame. The mean lift was **+1.06**, ranging from +0.9 to +1.4. Finish 2
removed what the first judges held against finish 1: the clipping, the halos, the bleached LED and the grain.

Every judge on finish 2 named one remaining cost. The shoulder compresses the brightest diffuse areas: the top of a
lit face under the eave, or a sun patch on a wall. They hold less modelling than they did. Two settings trade this
against the lift, and both are on the critic's card: `finish.white_out` and `finish.white_in`. Light can trade it
too, by keeping the brightest face nearer the white paint's median.

## What changed

The engine has a finish stage (`studio/engine/finish.py`; see the engine README, "The finish"). Each shot turns it
on with a calibrated `white_in` when its next critic round is staged. That way every round's tests are measured on
the frame its critic saw: rounds already in flight were measured before the finish and render without it.
